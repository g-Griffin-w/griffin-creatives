HOW OUTFITTERS GET MORE
GOOGLE REVIEWS.

Twenty minutes to set up, two minutes per hunter after that. The review request message, the QR card, the one reminder, and word-for-word replies to good and bad reviews.

Why reviews decide bookings

A guided elk hunt is expensive, it happens once a year at best, and it is hard to undo. The hunter cannot try you out first. So they look for proof that other hunters were treated well, and they read your reviews next to your competitor's.

The problem is that a hunter who had a great week almost never thinks to leave a review on their own. They get home, they are tired, and the trip turns into a story. Reviews that show up unprompted tend to come from whoever was upset. Asking is the only thing that fixes that.

What a good review looks like

Not "great guys, 5 stars." The reviews that move a hunter off the fence mention specifics: the type of hunt, the guide's name, how camp was and what happened. "Jake put us on a bull by day two and packed it out himself" does more work than ten generic reviews. You can encourage specifics without scripting anything, and the message below does exactly that.

Step 1: Get your direct review link

In your Google Business Profile, find "Ask for reviews" (Google sometimes calls it "Get more reviews"). It gives you a short link that opens the review box directly, so the hunter does not have to search for you. Copy it and test it on your phone. If you have not set up your profile yet, start with the Google Business Profile setup guide.

Step 2: Turn the link into a QR code

Paste the link into any free QR code generator and print the code on a card, a sticker or a small sign for the gear bag or the camp wall. Hand it over at drop-off while the hunter is still happy and the trip is still fresh.

Step 3: Save the message

Save this in your phone so you can send it in under a minute. Texts usually get tapped fastest. Send an email instead if email is the only contact you have.

TEXT OR EMAIL TEMPLATE

Hey [first name], hope you are settled back in. It was a pleasure having you in camp.

If you have a minute, a quick Google review helps other hunters find us. If you want to mention which hunt you were on and who guided you, that is the stuff other hunters look for: [your review link]

Thank you. [Your name]

For the in-person ask at drop-off, one sentence is enough:

AT DROP-OFF

If you have a minute when you are home, a Google review helps other hunters find us. Here is a card with the link.

ONE REMINDER, A WEEK LATER

Hey [first name], no pressure at all. If you get a chance to leave that review, here is the link again: [your review link]. Thanks again for hunting with us.

The schedule for every hunt

  • At drop-off: hand over the card and say the one sentence.
  • Two or three days later: send the text or email.
  • A week after that: send one gentle reminder, and only one. Then let it go.

The rules that keep you out of trouble

  • Ask everyone, not only the hunters you think were happy. Google's policies prohibit asking for positive reviews only.
  • Never offer a discount, a gift or anything free in exchange for a review. That is against Google's rules and can get your reviews removed.
  • If a hunt went sideways, call that hunter first. Listen. Then send the same message everyone else gets.
  • Elk hunting is hard. A hunter who did not fill a tag can still have had a great trip. Do not skip them.

Policies change, so read Google's current review guidelines before you start.

Reply to every review

Answer each review within a couple of days, using the hunter's first name and mentioning something specific. Hunters reading a bad review care more about how you responded than about what the review said.

REPLY TO A GOOD REVIEW

Thanks, Mike. That bugle on the ridge on day three was something. Glad Jake got you into that bull. Hope to see you again in September.

REPLY TO A BAD REVIEW

Mike, I am sorry the trip did not go the way you hoped. I would like to hear more and make it right if I can. Please call me at [number] when you get a chance.

Stay calm, stay factual and move the conversation offline. Do not argue in public.

Track it in a simple sheet

Keep five columns: hunter, hunt dates, text sent, reminder sent, reviewed. It takes five minutes a week and it is how you notice the one hunter you forgot.

Check yourself

  • Review link copied and tested on a phone
  • QR card printed and in the gear bag
  • Message saved on your phone with your link in it
  • Every review answered within a couple of days
  • Tracking sheet started

Common questions

How do I ask hunters for Google reviews?

Get your direct review link from your Google Business Profile, then send every hunter a short text or email two or three days after they leave camp, with one reminder a week later. Handing over a QR code card at drop-off helps too.

Can I offer a discount for a review?

No. Offering a discount, a gift or anything free in exchange for a review is against Google's rules and can get your reviews removed.

Should I only ask the hunters who had a good time?

No. Ask everyone. Google's policies prohibit asking for positive reviews only. If a hunt went badly, call that hunter first, then send the same message everyone else gets.

How should I reply to a bad review?

Stay calm and factual, use the hunter's first name, say you would like to make it right and give a phone number so the conversation moves offline. People reading the review care how you responded.

When is the best time to ask for a review?

While the trip is fresh: hand over a QR card at drop-off, send the message two or three days later, and send one reminder a week after that.
