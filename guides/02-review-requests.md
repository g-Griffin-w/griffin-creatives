# Ask for Reviews After Every Hunt (the DIY version)

**Time:** 20 minutes to set up, 2 minutes per hunter after that.

## Why reviews decide bookings

Elk hunts are expensive, rare, and hard to undo. A hunter is trusting you with a week of leave, a license, and a lot of money, and they can't try you out first. So they look for proof that other hunters were treated well. They read your reviews, and they read your competitor's.

Here's the problem: a hunter who had a great time almost never thinks to leave a review on their own. They get home, they're tired, the trip fades into a story. The reviews that appear unprompted tend to skew toward whoever was upset. **Asking is the only thing that fixes that.**

## What a good review looks like

Not "great guys, 5 stars." The reviews that actually move a hunter off the fence mention specifics: *the hunt type, the guide's name, how camp was, what happened.* "Jake put us on a bull by day two and packed it out himself" does more work than ten generic reviews. You can encourage specifics without scripting anything, and the message below does it.

## Set it up once

**1. Get your direct review link.**
In your Google Business Profile dashboard, find **"Ask for reviews"** (Google sometimes calls it "Get more reviews"). It gives you a short link that opens your review box directly. Copy it. Test it on your phone so you know it lands where you expect.

**2. Turn it into a QR code.**
Paste the link into any free QR code generator and print the code on a card, a sticker, or a sign for the gear bag or the camp wall. Hand it over at drop-off while they're still happy and the trip is still in their head.

**3. Save the message in your phone.**

> Hey [first name], hope you're settled back in. It was a pleasure having you in camp. If you've got a minute, a quick Google review helps other hunters find us. If you want to mention which hunt you were on and who guided you, that's the stuff other hunters look for: [your link]
> Thank you. — [your name]

## Run it every hunt

**At drop-off:** hand them the card and say one sentence: "If you've got a minute when you're home, this helps other hunters find us."

**Two or three days later:** send the text. A text is usually the quickest to get a tap, but send an email too if that's the only contact you have.

**A week after that:** one gentle reminder, and only one. Then let it go.

## The rules that keep you out of trouble

- **Send it to everyone,** not only the hunters you think were happy. Google's policies prohibit selectively asking for positive reviews only.
- **Never offer a discount, gift, or free anything in exchange.** That's against Google's rules and can get your reviews removed.
- **If a hunt went sideways, call that hunter first.** Listen. Then send the same message everyone else gets.
- **Elk hunting is hard.** A hunter who didn't punch a tag can still have had a great trip. Don't skip them.

## Replying to reviews

Every review gets a reply within a couple of days, using their first name and mentioning something specific.

**For a good review:**
> Thanks, Mike. That bugle on the ridge on day 3 was something. Glad Jake got you into that bull. Hope to see you again in September.

**For a bad one:** stay calm, stay factual, and move it offline.
> Mike, I'm sorry the trip didn't go the way you hoped. I'd like to hear more and make it right if I can. Call me at [number] when you get a chance.

Hunters reading a bad review care more about how you responded than what the review said.

## Keep a simple tracking sheet

| Hunter | Hunt dates | Text sent | Reminder sent | Reviewed? |
|---|---|---|---|---|

Five minutes a week. It's how you notice the one hunter you forgot.
