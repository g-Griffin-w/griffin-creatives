# Three Emails That Bring Past Clients Back (the DIY version)

**Time:** about an hour to write, then five minutes per send.

## Why this is the cheapest booking you'll ever get

A past client has already done the hard part. They found you, trusted you, paid, hunted your country, and slept in your camp. Getting a brand-new hunter takes ads, a website, and weeks of back-and-forth. Getting a past client back takes one honest email at the right time.

Most outfitters never send it. The hunter goes home, the season ends, and the next time that hunter hears from them is never. The hunter doesn't forget you; they just book whoever reaches them first.

## Before you write: build one list

Put every past client in one spreadsheet: first name, last name, email, the year they hunted, and one detail you remember (the bull they shot, the weather, who they came with). That last column is what makes these emails sound like you wrote them yourself.

Send from your own email address. Plain text, no fancy design, no logo header. It should read like a note from you, because it is. A free email tool (MailerLite, Mailchimp) or a mail merge in Gmail or Outlook lets you send to a list without writing each one by hand, and still put their first name at the top.

## Email 1: The thank-you (within a week of their hunt ending)

**Goal:** stay in their head. **No ask in this email.**

> **Subject:** Thank you from [Camp name]
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Thanks for hunting with us. I keep coming back to [one specific moment: the bull at first light on day four, the long pack out, the night the bugling wouldn't quit].
>
> I'm sorting through photos and will send you the good ones this week. Safe travels home.
>
> [Your name]

Here's what it looks like filled in:

> Hi Mike, thanks for hunting with us. I keep coming back to that evening on the ridge when three bulls were screaming at each other and you didn't say a word for ten minutes. I'm sorting photos and I'll send the good ones this week. Safe travels. — Tom

## Email 2: The rebook (December or January)

**Goal:** get a date on the calendar before they've committed it somewhere else.

> **Subject:** Next season at [Camp name]: dates are open
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Dates for [year] are open. I've got [number] spots in [month range] and I'm giving past clients first pick before I open them up.
>
> A [deposit amount] deposit holds your spot. If you've got a buddy who's been wanting to try it, bring him. I'll hold two spots together.
>
> Reply here and I'll send details.
>
> [Your name]

**Only say "first pick" and the spot count if they're true.** Your clients will notice.

## Email 3: The application reminder (spring, draw states only)

**Goal:** this is the email that earns trust. You're helping them even if they don't book.

> **Subject:** Tag deadline is [date]
>
> Hi [first name],
>
> Quick heads up: the [state] elk application deadline is [date]. [Link to your agency's application page]
>
> If you draw, I'm holding [dates] for you. If you don't, let me know and we'll figure out next year.
>
> [Your name]

## The one follow-up

If someone doesn't reply to Email 2 after about ten days, send one short line: *"Wanted to make sure this didn't get buried. Dates are still open if you're thinking about [year]."* Then stop.

## Get your dates right

Deadlines move year to year, so always confirm them on the agency's own site.

- **Oregon (ODFW):** controlled elk hunt applications are due May 15, with results around mid-June.
- **Idaho (IDFG):** the application window runs May 1 to June 5, with results from mid-June to early July, and a second drawing in August for leftover tags.
- **Montana, Washington, California:** check Montana FWP, WDFW, or CDFW directly. License types and deadlines differ, so don't guess.

## Mistakes to avoid

- Writing like a brochure. "We offer premier guided hunting experiences" gets deleted. "I keep thinking about that bull on day four" gets read.
- Making Email 1 a sales email. The thank-you has to be a thank-you.
- Sending the rebook email in May. By then they've booked, or they've drawn somewhere else.
- Burying the deposit and date info. Say the number and the month in the email.
