SET UP YOUR GOOGLE BUSINESS PROFILE.

The free listing that decides whether a hunter calls you or the outfitter next to you.

Time: 45 min + verificationCost: FreeDo it: Once, then 10 min a month

Why this is the highest-leverage hour you'll spend. A hunter planning a once-a-year trip doesn't call the first outfitter they hear about. They search. For a lot of local searches Google shows a map with three businesses above everything else, including your website, and those three listings get most of the calls.

In the 20 seconds a hunter spends on your listing, they're trying to answer five questions: Is this a real operation? Where's camp? Who's guiding me? What does the country look like? Do guys actually kill elk here? A filled-out profile answers all five before they click through.

1

Claim it, and check for duplicates

Search your business name on Google Maps. Then search your old business names and your phone number. Outfitters change names, add partners, and move, and it's common to find two or three stale listings for the same operation. Duplicates split your reviews and confuse Google about which one is real.

  • If a listing exists, click “Claim this business.”
  • If you find duplicates, claim the real one, then use “Suggest an edit” to mark the others closed or duplicate.
  • If nothing exists, go to business.google.com and add it.
2

Use your real business name

Use the name that's on your sign and your website: “Bear Paw Outfitters,” not “Bear Paw Outfitters | Best Montana Elk Hunting Guide.”

Watch outStuffing keywords into the business name is against Google’s guidelines and is one of the most common reasons listings get suspended.
3

Verify

Google offers some mix of postcard, phone call, text, email, or video. Postcards can take a week or more and go to a physical address, so make sure someone actually opens the mail there.

TipStart verification first, then finish the rest of this guide while you wait.
4

Set a service area, not a storefront

Hunters don't drive to your ranch to browse. You're a service-area business, which means you tell Google where you work instead of where you stand.

  • Hide your street address. It matters if your camp is also your home.
  • Add the regions you actually hunt: counties, the national forest, unit areas. Not just your mailing-address town.
5

Pick your categories

Open the primary category dropdown and search “hunting,” “outfitter,” and “guide.” Pick the closest match.

Then do the check most people skip: search “elk hunting outfitter [your region]” and see which category the top three results use. That's what Google is rewarding in your area.

TipAdd secondary categories only if they’re true. If you also run fishing trips, add that. Don’t add things you don’t do.
6

Write the description

You get 750 characters. Put the important details first, because the preview cuts off. Say what's included and what isn't. Hunters read that line twice.

Description template
[Camp name] is a family-run elk outfitter guiding [fully guided / semi-guided] [archery and rifle] hunts in [region / units] since [year]. We hunt [public land / private ranch / wilderness] out of a [pack-in camp / lodge], with [number] hunters per week and [ratio] guides. Hunts include [lodging, meals, guide, pack-out, meat care]. Licenses, tags, and transport to camp are not included. Call or book online to reserve [year] dates.
7

List each hunt as its own service

Don't make one vague “Hunting” entry. Create a service for each real hunt with a name, a short description, and a price or “from $X.”

Service entry example
5-Day Guided Archery Elk Hunt, September Fully guided, [number] hunters per guide, pack-in camp. Includes meals, lodging, guide, and meat care. From $[price].

Add real photos

Watch outNo stock photos, ever. Hunters can tell, and Google rewards real, recent photos. Add a few new ones each month through the season.
9

Make your contact details match

Phone, website, and business name should read the same way on your profile, your website, Facebook, and anywhere else you're listed. If you're in the backcountry and can't pick up, say so in the description and set up a voicemail that promises a callback within a day.

10

Keep it alive

  • Post when you have openings, season updates, or a fresh photo. Two lines is fine.
  • Reply to every review within a couple of days. (Guide 4 shows how.)
  • Update seasonal details each January: dates, prices, availability.

A profile with recent activity looks alive to Google and to hunters. One that sat untouched for two years looks abandoned.

The mistakes that cost outfitters the most

  • Keyword-stuffed business name (suspension risk)
  • Leaving your home address visible
  • Claiming categories that aren’t true
  • Stock photos or brochure shots instead of real ones
  • Never replying to reviews
  • Phone numbers that differ between your site and your profile
