THREE EMAILS THAT BRING PAST CLIENTS BACK.

The cheapest booking you will ever get is a hunter who already trusts you. Here is exactly what to send, and when.

Time: 1 hourPer send: 5 minCost: Free

Why this is the cheapest booking you'll ever get. A past client has already done the hard part. They found you, trusted you, paid, hunted your country, and slept in your camp. Getting a brand-new hunter takes ads, a website, and weeks of back-and-forth. Getting a past client back takes one honest email at the right time.

Most outfitters never send it. The hunter goes home, the season ends, and the next time that hunter hears from them is never. The hunter doesn't forget you. They just book whoever reaches them first.

1

Build one list

Put every past client in one spreadsheet: first name, last name, email, the year they hunted, and one detail you remember (the bull they shot, the weather, who they came with). That last column is what makes these sound like you wrote them yourself.

TipSend from your own email address, in plain text, with no logo header. It should read like a note from you. A free email tool (MailerLite, Mailchimp) or a mail merge in Gmail or Outlook lets you send to a list and still put their first name at the top.
2

Email 1: the thank-you

Send it within a week of their hunt ending. Goal: stay in their head. No ask in this email.

Template
Subject: Thank you from [Camp name]Hi [first name], Thanks for hunting with us. I keep coming back to [one specific moment: the bull at first light on day four, the long pack out, the night the bugling wouldn't quit]. I'm sorting through photos and will send you the good ones this week. Safe travels home. [Your name]

Filled in

Example
Subject: Thank you from Bear Paw OutfittersHi Mike, Thanks for hunting with us. I keep coming back to that evening on the ridge when three bulls were screaming at each other and you didn't say a word for ten minutes. I'm sorting photos and I'll send the good ones this week. Safe travels. Tom
3

Email 2: the rebook

Send it in December or January. Goal: get a date on the calendar before they've committed it somewhere else.

Template
Subject: Next season at [Camp name]: dates are openHi [first name], Dates for [year] are open. I've got [number] spots in [month range] and I'm giving past clients first pick before I open them up. A [deposit amount] deposit holds your spot. If you've got a buddy who's been wanting to try it, bring him. I'll hold two spots together. Reply here and I'll send details. [Your name]
Watch outOnly say “first pick” and the spot count if they’re true. Your clients will notice.
4

Email 3: the application reminder

Send it in spring, for draw states only. This is the email that earns trust. You're helping them even if they don't book.

TipTiming: send this two to three weeks before your state’s deadline. In Wyoming that means mid-January (nonresident deadline Feb 2). In Montana, mid-March (April 1). In Colorado, mid-March (April 7 in 2026). In Utah, early April (April 23 in 2026). In Oregon, mid-April (May 15). In California, mid-May (June 2).
Template
Subject: Tag deadline is [date]Hi [first name], Quick heads up: the [state] elk application deadline is [date]. [Link to your agency's application page] If you draw, I'm holding [dates] for you. If you don't, let me know and we'll figure out next year. [Your name]
5

The one follow-up

If someone doesn't reply to Email 2 after about ten days, send one line, then stop.

Follow-up line
Wanted to make sure this didn't get buried. Dates are still open if you're thinking about [year].
6

Get your dates right

Deadlines move year to year, so always confirm them on the agency's own site.

  • Oregon (ODFW): controlled elk hunt applications are due May 15, with results around mid-June.
  • Idaho (IDFG): the application window runs May 1 to June 5, with results from mid-June to early July, and a second drawing in August for leftover tags.
  • Montana (FWP): applications open March 1. Elk combination licenses and elk permits are due April 1 (11:45 pm Mountain), with the drawing in mid-April. The Elk B drawing is due June 1, with results in mid-June. Montana runs earlier than Oregon or Idaho.
  • Washington (WDFW): special hunt permits, including elk, opened April 20 and closed May 27 in 2026, with results in June. Multi-season deer and elk tags are sold December 1 through March 31, with results in April. Applications for 2027 open December 1, 2026.
  • California (CDFW): elk tags come from the Big Game Drawing, which opens April 15 and is due midnight June 2 every year, with results ten business days later (about mid-June).
  • Wyoming (WGFD): applications opened January 2. Nonresident elk applications are due February 2, the earliest of any state here. Resident elk applications are due June 1.
  • Colorado (CPW): the 2026 primary draw opened March 1 and closed April 7 at 8 pm Mountain. Unfilled licenses go to a secondary draw, due the last business day of June.
  • Utah (DWR): the 2026 big game drawing ran March 19 to April 23, with results by May 31. Remaining permits went on sale July 7.

These are the 2026 cycle dates, checked on each agency's own site in October 2026. Montana, California, Colorado and Utah haven't posted 2027 dates yet, so confirm yours each year.

Mistakes to avoid

  • Writing like a brochure. “We offer premier guided hunting experiences” gets deleted. “I keep thinking about that bull on day four” gets read.
  • Making Email 1 a sales email. The thank-you has to be a thank-you.
  • Sending the rebook email in May. By then they’ve booked, or drawn somewhere else.
  • Burying the deposit and dates. Say the number and the month.
