# Your Elk Season Marketing Calendar (the DIY version)

**Time:** 30 minutes to fill in, then follow it all year.

## Why most outfitters scramble every August

The usual pattern: you're busy guiding from September through November. You come home tired in December and don't think about next year. Spring is when you finally notice you've got three open spots in September, and now you're discounting, posting on Facebook, and calling old clients you haven't spoken to in a year.

The fix is to stop treating marketing as something you do when you feel behind. Elk hunters follow a predictable year: they apply in spring, they find out in early summer, they book in the months around it, and they hunt in the fall. If you show up at the same predictable moments every year, you're in front of them when they're deciding.

## One idea that changes how you plan

If you're in a state where elk tags come from a draw, a hunter often **can't commit to you until they know whether they drew.** That splits your year into two phases:

- **Before results:** you're selling "apply, and if you draw I'm holding dates for you."
- **After results:** you're selling to people who have a tag in hand and a week of leave to spend. This is the most motivated hunter you'll ever talk to.

Most outfitters treat these as one phase and talk to everyone the same way. Don't.

## Step 1: Fill in your four dates

Look these up on your state agency's site (ODFW, IDFG, Montana FWP, WDFW, or CDFW) and write them down:

| Date | Yours |
|---|---|
| Application deadline | ______ |
| Draw results | ______ |
| Season opens | ______ |
| Season closes | ______ |

For reference: Oregon's elk application deadline is May 15, with results around mid-June. Idaho's window runs May 1 to June 5, with results from mid-June to early July and a second drawing in August for leftover tags. Dates shift, so confirm yours every year, and note that archery and rifle seasons often run different dates.

## Step 2: Follow the year

**December: the thank-you and the rebook.**
Send thank-you emails to this year's hunters, then the rebooking email. This is also when you look at which dates sold out and which didn't. That tells you what to push.

**January and February: the quiet push.**
Update your website and Google profile with next season's dates, prices, and your best photos from this year. Send the rebooking email to anyone who didn't answer. Example post: *"2026 dates are open. Archery and rifle weeks available. Past clients get first pick through January."*

**March and April: the application reminder.**
A few weeks before your state's deadline, email every past client the application reminder. You're helping them whether they book or not, and the ones who apply remember who reminded them.

**May and June: the deadline and the results.**
This is when every hunter is paying attention. Post on your Google profile around the deadline. The week results come out, reach out to everyone who drew: *"Congrats on the tag. I'm holding [dates] for you."* This is your highest-converting week of the year.

**July and August: the confirmation.**
Confirm your hunters, send prep info (gear list, fitness, what to expect), and post pre-season photos. This is also when you check for leftover or second-draw tags and use them to fill any openings that are left.

**September through November: the season.**
Take photos as you go (ask permission). Send review requests within a few days of each hunt. Note any cancellations, because a late opening can be filled with a last-minute post and a call to your list.

Then it starts over in December.

## Step 3: Put it on your actual calendar

A guide in a folder doesn't help you. Open your calendar and create recurring reminders for each month's task, with the email or post you need attached. Twenty minutes now means you don't have to remember any of it.
