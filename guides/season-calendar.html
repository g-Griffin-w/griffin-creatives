YOUR ELK SEASON MARKETING CALENDAR.

Stop scrambling to fill September in August. Show up at the same moments every year, when hunters are deciding.

Time: 30 minThen: Follow it all yearCost: Free

Why most outfitters scramble every August. You're busy guiding from September through November. You come home tired in December and don't think about next year. Spring is when you finally notice three open spots in September, and now you're discounting, posting on Facebook, and calling old clients you haven't spoken to in a year.

The fix is to stop marketing when you feel behind. Elk hunters follow a predictable year: they apply in spring, find out in early summer, book in the months around it, and hunt in the fall. If you show up at the same predictable moments every year, you're in front of them when they're deciding.

One idea that changes how you plan

In a state where elk tags come from a draw, a hunter often can't commit to you until they know whether they drew. That splits your year in two:

1

Fill in your four dates

Look these up on your state agency's site (ODFW, IDFG, Montana FWP, WDFW, or CDFW) and type them in. They save in this browser so you don't lose them.

TipFor reference: Oregon’s elk application deadline is May 15, with results around mid-June. Idaho’s window runs May 1 to June 5, with results from mid-June to early July and a second drawing in August. Dates shift, so confirm yours every year.

Follow the year, month by month

December: the thank-you and the rebook

Send thank-you emails to this year's hunters, then the rebooking email (see Guide 1). Look at which dates sold out and which didn't. That tells you what to push.

January and February: the quiet push

Update your website and Google profile with next season's dates, prices, and your best photos. Email anyone who didn't answer.

Example post
2026 dates are open. Archery and rifle weeks available. Past clients get first pick through January.

March and April: the application reminder

A few weeks before your state's deadline, email every past client. You're helping them whether they book or not, and the ones who apply remember who reminded them.

May and June: the deadline and the results

Every hunter is paying attention. Post on your profile around the deadline. The week results come out, reach out to everyone who drew. This is your highest-converting week of the year.

Message to a hunter who drew
Congrats on the tag, [first name]. I'm holding [dates] for you. Want me to send details?

July and August: the confirmation

Confirm your hunters, send prep info (gear list, fitness, what to expect), post pre-season photos, and check for leftover or second-draw tags to fill any openings that are left.

September through November: the season

Take photos as you go (ask permission). Send review requests within a few days of each hunt (see Guide 4). Note any cancellations, because a late opening can be filled with a quick post and a call to your list.

Then it starts over in December.

4

Put it on your actual calendar

A guide in a folder doesn't help you. Open your calendar and create a recurring reminder for each month's task, with the email or post you need attached. Twenty minutes now means you don't have to remember any of it.
