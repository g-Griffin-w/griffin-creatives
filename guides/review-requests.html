ASK FOR REVIEWS AFTER EVERY HUNT.

Happy hunters rarely review on their own. Here is the two-minute habit that fixes that.

Setup: 20 minPer hunter: 2 minCost: Free

Why reviews decide bookings. Elk hunts are expensive, rare, and hard to undo. A hunter is trusting you with a week of leave, a license, and a lot of money, and they can't try you out first. So they look for proof that other hunters were treated well. They read your reviews, and they read your competitor's.

A hunter who had a great time almost never thinks to leave one. They get home, they're tired, the trip fades into a story. The reviews that show up unprompted tend to skew toward whoever was upset. Asking is the only thing that fixes that.

Good to knowWhat a good review looks like. Not “great guys, 5 stars.” The ones that move a hunter off the fence mention specifics: the hunt type, the guide’s name, how camp was, what happened. “Jake put us on a bull by day two and packed it out himself” does more than ten generic reviews. The message below nudges toward specifics without scripting anything.
// What you'll do

1

Get your direct review link

In your Google Business Profile dashboard, find “Ask for reviews” (Google sometimes calls it “Get more reviews”). It gives you a short link that opens your review box directly. Copy it, then test it on your phone so you know it lands where you expect.

2

Turn it into a QR code

Paste the link into any free QR code generator and print the result on a card, a sticker, or a sign for the gear bag or the camp wall. Hand it over at drop-off while they're still happy.

3

Save the message

Put this in your phone's notes so it's one tap away. Swap in your name, the hunter's name, and your link.

Review request message
Hey [first name], hope you're settled back in. It was a pleasure having you in camp. If you've got a minute, a quick Google review helps other hunters find us. If you want to mention which hunt you were on and who guided you, that's the stuff other hunters look for: [your link] Thank you. — [your name]
4

Run it every hunt

At drop-offHand them the card. Say one sentence: “If you’ve got a minute when you’re home, this helps other hunters find us.”
2–3 days laterSend the text. A text is usually the quickest to get a tap. Email too if that's all you have.
A week afterOne gentle reminder, and only one. Then let it go.
5

Follow the rules

RuleSend it to everyone, not only the hunters you think were happy. Google’s policies prohibit selectively asking for positive reviews only.
Watch outNever offer a discount, gift, or free anything in exchange for a review. That’s against Google’s rules and can get your reviews removed.
Good practiceIf a hunt went sideways, call that hunter first. Listen. Then send the same message everyone else gets.
Good practiceElk hunting is hard. A hunter who didn’t punch a tag can still have had a great trip. Don’t skip them.
6

Reply to every review

Every review gets a reply within a couple of days, using their first name and something specific. Hunters reading a bad review care more about how you responded than what it said.

Reply to a good review
Thanks, Mike. That bugle on the ridge on day 3 was something. Glad Jake got you into that bull. Hope to see you again in September.
Reply to a bad review (stay calm, stay factual, move it offline)
Mike, I'm sorry the trip didn't go the way you hoped. I'd like to hear more and make it right if I can. Call me at [number] when you get a chance.
7

Track it

Five minutes a week. It's how you notice the one hunter you forgot.
