# Competitor breakdown: marketing for hunting outfitters (Oct 20, 2026)

Built from each competitor's own website plus web searches. **Almost every number below is the competitor's own claim, not verified.** Several pages show placeholder case studies or no prices. Where a page didn't say something, I say so instead of guessing.

## The landscape in one view
| Type | Who | What they sell | Price they show |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Outfitter-focused agencies** | Outfitter Marketing Pros, Riverrise, SearchPod, OxBow, Outfitter Logic | Websites, SEO, ads, email, reviews, Google profile | OMP $1,000–$3,500/mo; SearchPod $1,000/mo; others hide it |
| **Hunting/outdoor creative agencies** | Modern Outdoor Media, Deer Camp Digital, Coutts Agency, Weicks Media, Big Canoe Digital | Brand, sites, video, social, ads | None shown; Modern Outdoor says projects start in the low five figures |

**Closest to us:** Riverrise (Bozeman, MT), Outfitter Marketing Pros, and SearchPod.

## Profiles

### Riverrise (Bozeman, Montana): our closest direct competitor
- **Who:** outfitters, guides, lodges. Also fishing guides and lodges.
- **Offer:** content, CRM and marketing automation, paid ads, SEO, social, web design. Hunting-specific: SEO for species and area searches, Google Ads timed to draws and booking windows, email to past clients and waitlists, **Google Business Profile management**, hunt-focused websites, review systems, instant lead follow-up, monthly reporting "in plain language."
- **Pricing:** none shown. A custom plan comes after a free audit.
- **Proof:** five-star Google reviews quoted (no numbers), brand logos (YETI, Shimano, Eddie Bauer, NRS), memberships in **Montana Outfitters and Guides Association**, Rocky Mountain Elk Foundation and Mule Deer Foundation. No case-study figures.
- **Strategies:** a **free marketing audit including a "100-point Google Business Profile audit,"** a **"2027 Booking Scorecard"** resource, a page for each Montana town (Billings, Missoula, Kalispell, Helena, Big Sky and more) for local search, and campaigns for Wyoming, Idaho, South Dakota and North Dakota tied to each state's application calendar.
- **Overlap with us:** Montana, Wyoming, Idaho; draw-date timing; Google profile; past-client email; a free audit. They are doing a lot of what we plan to.

### Outfitter Marketing Pros
- **Who:** outdoor businesses only: fishing guides, lodges, hunting outfitters, guest ranches.
- **Offer:** local and national SEO, AI-search visibility, Meta ads, Google Ads, website design, a custom CRM, branding.
- **Pricing (public):** **Base $1,000/mo, Peak $2,250/mo, Pro $3,500/mo.** All three list the same features; the page doesn't show what differs.
- **Proof:** "281 trips booked in 8 months from organic search" (a fly-fishing client), "11.1x return on ad spend over 6 months of Google Ads" (a fishing client), claims "50+ other outfitters," about 20 outfitter logos. Most named results are fishing, not hunting.
- **Strategies:** free strategy call, a **free AI visibility score** with a PDF report, a package-picker quiz, a bi-weekly newsletter ("The Guide Line"), a Google Partner badge, partnerships with outdoor associations, a "book direct instead of through third-party platforms" message.

### SearchPod (North Vancouver and Toronto)
- **Who:** outfitters and guide services (deer, elk, waterfowl, turkey) who want direct bookings and less reliance on directories.
- **Offer:** Google and Meta ads, SEO, email, Google profile, reviews, **AI-search optimization**, booking and call tracking (instant text-back on missed calls), websites.
- **Pricing (public):** **$1,000 a month platform plan,** $0 setup, month to month, **30-day "if you're not satisfied, you don't pay" guarantee**; ads/SEO performance plans priced on results.
- **Proof:** no named case studies. Sample sites are mostly other niches. Says Google Ads can bring bookings in weeks and SEO takes 3–6 months.
- **Strategies:** transparent pricing, a free proposal in one business day, a free audit, "your ad accounts, domain and Business Profile stay yours," per-booking attribution.

### OxBow (Michigan)
- **Who:** outfitters, guides, shooting preserves, ranches, lodges; high-ticket trips (their FAQ uses a $9,000 guided elk hunt as an example).
- **Offer:** Meta ads, Google and YouTube ads, email, websites, brand and media, one-on-one business coaching, a "Marketing Diagnostic Tool," a members' community.
- **Pricing:** none shown; goes through "Get my booking plan."
- **Proof:** "200+ booked trips and 8x ROAS" (Fishound Expeditions, fishing), "5x bookings" (Bust Em Up Outfitters), a "completely booked season" (Rogue Texan Outfitters). Some case-study links go nowhere.
- **Strategies:** positions against **booking agents who take 10–15%** by building the outfitter's own audience; qualify leads with transparent pricing in ads; follow up after sport shows; FAQ content that answers outfitter objections; a free diagnostic tool.

### Modern Outdoor Media (Missouri)
- **Who:** outfitters plus hunting gear brands, dog programs, conservation groups, land brands.
- **Offer:** websites, SEO, brand identity, content, social, paid media, PR, influencer work.
- **Pricing:** none public; project work "starts in the low five figures," retainers priced after a discovery call.
- **Proof:** no numbers; three case studies are placeholders. Credibility comes from the founders being hunters and dog breeders.
- **Strategies:** authenticity over stock imagery, "no pitch deck," won't take competing clients in the same market. No free audit or lead magnet.

### Deer Camp Digital (Austin)
- **Who:** ranch real estate, agriculture, conservation groups, outfitters, outdoor brands.
- **Offer:** sites, SEO, social, video, ads, email, AI automation, and **RanchOS**, its own listing platform for ranch brokers.
- **Pricing and proof:** no prices and no result numbers. It doesn't say how it handles ad-policy limits on hunting.
- **Strategies:** "we only work in industries we actually know," a strategy-call CTA.

### Outfitter Logic (Bismarck, North Dakota)
- **Who:** hunting guides and outfitters, mostly deer, waterfowl, pheasant.
- **Offer:** SEO websites, Google profile, social, content, online booking calendars.
- **Pricing:** none shown ("extremely affordable").
- **Proof:** "hundreds of outfitter websites" over 20 years, three clients' traffic screenshots, a client with a 4-year waiting list.
- **Strategies:** long track record, referrals, phone-first sales; argues sport shows produce poor leads.

### Others (shallower look)
- **Weicks Media:** outfitter websites, virtual lodge tours, custom booking features, "20+ years." No prices or numbers.
- **The Coutts Agency (Tennessee):** firearms and hunting marketing, a free playbook for outfitters, ads via a platform it says avoids the usual restrictions (**I'd treat that claim with caution**; it may break platform rules).
- **Big Canoe Digital, Inflow, HUNTER Digital, 3plains:** appear in searches with SEO, ads and email services. I couldn't load Big Canoe's pages; no prices found.

### Marketplaces and software
- **Guidefitter:** free outfitter pages and a trip finder; a paid Guidefitter-hosted website upgrade. Ranking depends on how complete and active the profile is. I couldn't find prices or lead fees.
- **Mallard Bay (Louisiana):** booking and payments software with a free plan and paid plans from **$199/mo**, no commission on bookings through the outfitter's own site, a marketplace with fees on bookings it sources, and **managed ads and lead follow-up in growth packages**. A search result shows a $4.6M Series A.
- **Hunt Outfitter and Guide Pointer:** low-cost booking and scheduling tools for small outfitters.

## What seems to be working for them
1. **Specialization claims.** Almost every site says it only works in hunting and outdoors, and that its founders hunt. It builds trust fast.
2. **Free audits and tools as the hook.** Riverrise (100-point Google profile audit and a 2027 Booking Scorecard), SearchPod (free audit and proposal), Outfitter Marketing Pros (free AI visibility score), OxBow (diagnostic tool).
3. **Named results with numbers.** Outfitter Marketing Pros and OxBow lead with trips booked and return on ad spend, even though most are fishing clients.
4. **Public pricing and risk reversal.** SearchPod and Outfitter Marketing Pros show prices. SearchPod adds month to month and a 30-day guarantee. Most others hide prices.
5. **"Book direct, skip the middleman."** OxBow, SearchPod and Outfitter Marketing Pros pitch against booking agents and marketplace fees.
6. **Local and regional pages.** Riverrise has a page per Montana town.
7. **Association ties.** Riverrise lists MOGA, RMEF and Mule Deer Foundation; Outfitter Marketing Pros lists outdoor associations.
8. **AI-search optimization** is a new selling point for SearchPod, Outfitter Marketing Pros and the Coutts Agency.
9. **Follow-up automation and plain-language reports.**

## Where we're weak
- **No proof.** Every serious competitor leads with results or logos. We have none, so the founding-client offer matters.
- **Price.** Outfitter Marketing Pros starts at $1,000 and SearchPod is $1,000 with a guarantee. We're $2,800 without ads. We must show clearly what the extra price buys (elk-only focus, the rebooking and review systems, a tag-draw calendar).
- **Riverrise overlaps us directly** in Montana, Wyoming and Idaho and already offers a Google profile audit and draw-timed campaigns.
- **No AI-search work** yet, which three competitors already sell.

## Where there's room for us
1. **Elk-only, with real draw-date knowledge.** Our guides cover eight states' application windows. Competitors mostly say "timed to draws" without showing the dates.
2. **A multi-email course as the lead magnet.** Competitors offer a one-time audit or score. Ours teaches for a week and ends with a pitch.
3. **Publish our price.** Only two competitors do. We already do, and the site says no contracts.
4. **Position against booking-agent commissions.** OxBow does this. For an outfitter paying 10–15% (Lost Creek's site says 15% is added), direct rebooking has a clear dollar value.
5. **Our own proof content.** After the first clients, get real numbers instead of claims.

## Suggestions (for you to decide)
- Add a **free Google-profile audit and scorecard** as a visible offer on the site and in the guides (we have the checklist in `GOOGLE-AUDIT.md`).
- Collect **a testimonial and numbers from the first 1–3 founding clients** and put them on the homepage as early as possible.
- Decide whether to add a **lower entry plan** to compete with the $1,000 tier. (The site made a deliberate "single offer" choice on Oct 8.)
- Look at **AI-search visibility** as a feature once the core service is proven.
- **Don't copy the "ads without restrictions" claim** some competitors make.

## Day-to-day marketing: what they actually do (checked Oct 20, 2026)
Sources: their blogs, public Instagram and Facebook pages, Meta's Ad Library (active US ads), and Google's Ads Transparency Center. **Limits:** Instagram and Facebook only showed profile numbers and a few posts without logging in, so I couldn't measure posting frequency. The Ad Library matches ad text as well as advertiser names, so a missing advertiser isn't proof of no ads. Treat this as a snapshot.

### Paid ads
- **Only Mallard Bay (booking software) is running Meta ads aimed at outfitters.** About 9 active ads from July to September 2026, in several versions:
  - A customer testimonial video (1 min 9 sec) with "Just do it," plus a promise of "a real person you can call or text."
  - A "you're juggling a dozen different tools" list ad, run in separate versions for hunting outfitters and fishing charters.
  - A **positioning ad: "Not a marketplace… We don't rent you leads,"** which goes after Guidefitter-style listing sites.
  - A **fear-of-AI-search ad:** "your outfitter website… you're becoming invisible to AI search tools like ChatGPT and Gemini."
  - A short "Website, Ads, All-in-One" video.
  - Every ad ends with a "book a walkthrough" or "no pressure" button. Several versions of each ad are running, which means they are testing.
- **Outfitter Marketing Pros, Riverrise and OxBow: no active Meta ads found** in the Ad Library. Outfitter Marketing Pros and Riverrise: **0 Google ads** found in the Ads Transparency Center.
- Outfitter Marketing Pros' site has a **Facebook tracking pixel**, so it can retarget visitors.
- Other advertisers in the same results: Guidesly Pro (software for fishing charter guides) and Outfitter.AI (a free-website offer aimed at home-service businesses).
- **What outfitters' own ads look like** (this is what their hunter-facing ads do): Homestead Ranch (Colorado) runs "Colorado Trophy Elk Hunts" with "Get Quote" buttons and a "100% Success" claim; Montana's Broken Arrow Outfitting runs a simple "Elk, Mule Deer & Antelope" ad. Meta accepts guided-hunt ads.

### Organic and content
- **Outfitter Marketing Pros:**
  - **Blog:** about one post a week (Aug 26 to Sep 24, 2026), nearly all about AI search ("AEO vs GEO," "How to get your tour company recommended by ChatGPT"), and by the founder.
  - **Newsletter:** a bi-weekly email, "The Guide Line."
  - **Instagram:** 167 posts, 527 followers, but following 1,440 accounts, so it follows heavily to be noticed. Its highlights are "AI Assistant," "Testimonials" and "Marketing Book," and the bio links to a podcast episode.
  - **Facebook:** 272 followers. Posts are client testimonial videos and a short hook post: "Google ads work. They could be working for you, but you're not working with us… yet."
  - **Lead capture:** a separate page at discover.outfittermarketingpros.com and a free AI visibility score.
- **OxBow:** the Instagram account has 40 posts, 3,511 followers and follows only 45. Its highlights are its clients' brands (Field Team, STS, Bloop Bead Co., Gundog Outdoors), so most of its social work is gear and product brands, not outfitters.
- **SearchPod:** a blog of about 1.5 posts a week on general business marketing (not hunting): SEO, "GEO optimization," Google Ads, budgets. Offers a free proposal and "free marketing tools."
- **Riverrise:** I couldn't find a blog (the likely URL returned a 404) or social accounts from its site. Its lead magnets are the audit and the 2027 Booking Scorecard.
- **Outfitter Logic, Weicks Media:** no active content I could find; they rely on old sites and referrals.

### Patterns across all of them
1. **The agencies get leads from content and free tools.** Blogs, newsletters, audits and scores. Almost none pay for ads to outfitters.
2. **AI search is the hot topic.** Outfitter Marketing Pros' recent blog posts, SearchPod's content, and Mallard Bay's ads all push "be visible in ChatGPT."
3. **Testimonials and client-result videos** are the main social proof.
4. **Software companies advertise; agencies don't.** That means a paid channel aimed at outfitters is less crowded with service competitors.
5. **Everyone sells a call,** not a purchase: "book a walkthrough," "free strategy call," "free audit."

### What this means for us
- **Nobody I found is advertising a free multi-guide course to outfitters.** Our funnel is different from a one-off audit.
- **Meta works for ads aimed at outfitters.** It's another way to test, besides Google Ads, and Mallard Bay proves outfitters are reachable there. Worth a small test after the Google test.
- **Our own content could borrow:** a weekly post on a hot topic, a short testimonial video once we have one, and a clean landing page for free-offer traffic.
- **The AI-search angle is crowded** by the time we'd arrive. If we use it, tie it to elk and draw dates.
- **Don't copy the follow-1,400-accounts approach** as a growth plan. It's a low-quality signal.

### Not looked at yet
LinkedIn, YouTube channel posting frequency, TikTok, email (we'd need to sign up to see their emails), and Riverrise's and SearchPod's social accounts.
