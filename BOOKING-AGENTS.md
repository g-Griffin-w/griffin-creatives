# Booking agents for elk hunts — what we found (Oct 19, 2026)

Sources: web searches, each agent's own website, and a scan of the 296 websites in our lead list (233 reachable).
Everything below is from a public page. Contact details are as listed on the site; check them before calling.
**No agent here is verified as licensed, reputable or currently active.** Treat each as a lead to check.

## How agents work (so we pitch the right thing)
- An agent matches hunters to outfitters and is paid by the outfitter. The hunter usually pays the same price (NRA Hunter Leadership Forum article).
- **Not always.** Lost Creek Outfitters (WY, one of our leads) says: "A charge of 15% is added to the cost of any hunt booked through an agent or through a referral agency." So commissions exist and some outfitters pass them on.
- Agents hold the client relationship. A hunter booked by an agent may rebook through the agent. Our "email your past clients directly" message may not suit an agent's outfitters, so ask the outfitter first, and pitch the agent on filling their outfitters' open dates.
- R&K Hunting Company (WY/UT, one of our leads) lists "Booking Agent" as a way hunters hear about them on its inquiry form, so agents are a real booking source for outfitters like ours.

## Other places agents show up
- **Western Hunting & Conservation Expo** (Salt Lake City, Feb 11–14, 2027): ask for the exhibitor list in December.
- **Safari Club International convention:** has a booking-agent section in its exhibitor rules. Attendance generally needs SCI membership.
- **Guidefitter** (guidefitter.com): a marketplace of outfitters, useful for seeing who is listed.

## From our own lead list
Scanned 296 outfitter websites for "booking agent", "booked through", "consultant" and similar. 11 pages matched. Only three were useful:
- **Five Star Expeditions (WY)**: an agency, see above.
- **Lost Creek Outfitters (WY)**: charges 15% on agent bookings.
- **R&K Hunting Company (WY/UT)**: agents send them leads.
Most matches were just the word "booked" in deposit terms. **None of our leads named a specific agent they use.** Asking on calls is still the best way to get names.

Data quality note: the lead "Pathfinder Hunting & Fishing Consultants, Inc." (WY) has a website that actually belongs to an Indiana kayak company. The website on that lead is wrong.

## Suggested next steps
1. **Pull Five Star Expeditions out of the cold-email queue.** It's an agency, so the outfitter pitch doesn't fit. Call or email them with the agent version instead.
2. **Call 3 agents first:** Outdoors International (the outfitter page suggests they want more outfitters), Five Star (also an outfitter) and Western Hunt Co. (Tyler Ferris is a one-person outfitter-consultant).
3. **Pitch:** "We help your outfitters fill open dates for next season. Can I send you the free guides to pass to them?" Don't pitch rebooking past clients directly.
4. **Ask on every outfitter call:** "Do you work with any booking agents?"
